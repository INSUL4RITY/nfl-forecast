Week {{WEEK}} matchups

Each game side by side: every offence against the defence it faces, special teams, and coaching tendencies. Pick a game, or use the cards at the bottom of this section.

How to read the expected values. "Expected in this matchup" adds the offence's and the defence's opponent-adjusted effects to the league average, plus home field. Each effect is first pulled towards the league average in proportion to how little data supports it (empirical-Bayes shrinkage), so a unit with few plays or noisy results moves the expectation very little. Confidence describes how much of each team's own record survives that shrinkage: moderate, low, very low, or no signal yet. Nothing here is a score or a result forecast, {{CAL_NOTE}}. Coaching labels describe what each staff has done in 2026 on small samples: 20-40 fourth-down decisions per team.

EPA and DVOA are different measures. EPA (expected points added) values each play by the change in nflfastR's expected points from before to after the snap, from down, distance, field position and time. It is open, play-by-play and unadjusted unless an adjustment is applied. DVOA (FTN) compares each play's success with a league baseline for the same situation, then adjusts for opponent and removes some luck-driven outcomes. DVOA is proprietary, is not in nflverse and is not shown here. The opponent-adjusted columns are EPA and success rate adjusted by a published regression (Method), not DVOA.

Efficiency

Garbage time: how much of each offence's production came in lopsided game states. "Flatter it" means the raw yards, points and EPA look better than the team has played in competitive snaps; "understate it" means the reverse. Every rating in this report uses all snaps.

Drives and scoring

Per-drive rates remove differences in the number of possessions. Points over start EP measures what a unit did with the field position it was given.

Passing

Quarterback and protection are separated where the data allows: hit/sack versus clean-pocket EPA, sacks per pressure (partly the quarterback), drops and YAC over expected (receivers). How often each defence plays man, zone, single-high or split-safety comes from Next Gen Stats (Defence section); results split by coverage are not shown, because they did not hold up in testing.

Quarterbacks

Per quarterback, not per team: a team's totals can mix several passers. Composite = 50% opponent-adjusted EPA/dropback, 30% CPOE, 20% interception-worthy rate (lower is better). This weighting is a stated judgement, not a fitted model.

Quarterback situations: where team figures mix passers

Team passing figures elsewhere include every quarterback who played. This table splits them, so a returning starter or a backup can be judged on his own games. Status comes from the latest roster and injury report.

Offensive players: usage, game logs and rankings

Who gets the ball for each offence: targets, carries and their share of the team's, the last three games beside the season, and each player's status. A player now out who took a large share of the targets or carries matters more than his snap share suggests. Shares of the work settle quickly; per-target and per-carry efficiency is mostly noise over a few games (see Method).

Rushing

Designed runs, quarterback designed runs and scrambles are kept apart. Yards before contact points to blocking; yards after contact and rush yards over expected point to the ball carrier. Both are imperfect splits.

Blitzing and pressure

Three different things. Blitz rate is how often a defence sends five or more rushers (FTN charting). Pressure rate is how often the quarterback is hurried, hit or sacked (PFR, game totals). Sack rate is sacks per dropback. A defence can blitz a lot without pressure, or create pressure with four and keep seven in coverage.

Individual pass rushers and coverage defenders

Who makes each defence's pressure and who gets targeted in coverage (PFR). A team whose pressure comes from one or two players loses much more when one of them is out than its team pressure rate suggests.

Special teams and coaching

CoachingSpecial teams

Coaching: who decides what

Each tendency is credited to the person who owns that decision. The head coach owns game management (fourth downs, two-point tries, end of half) even when he calls no plays; the play-callers own the play choices; the special-teams coordinator owns the units, with the head coach making the risky calls. These are observed decisions, not judgements of coaching quality.

The fourth-down reference compares each decision with historical average outcomes (2021-25), not with a win-probability model.

Turnovers, margin and sustainability

Expected values use FTN's interception-worthy charting and fumble counts. A large gap between actual and expected margin suggests regression is possible, not certain.

Luck, close games and regression candidates

Close-game records are not treated as luck on their own: quarterback quality, coaching and late-game execution all affect them. The categories are under Team profiles.

Penalties: offensive and defensive discipline

Counts are accepted penalties. Pre-snap fouls are the most clearly controllable. Pass interference, holding and illegal contact also come with aggressive coverage, and some calls are judgement calls by the officials.

Workload, possession and defensive fatigue

How much each offence leaves its defence on the field, and whether that has measurably hurt defences.

Rest, travel and body clock

Week {{WEEK}} schedule context for every team, then the historical evidence (2002-25 regular seasons) behind it. Effects are measured as points beyond what each team's season strength predicts, using leave-one-game-out ratings.

Strength of schedule and quality of opposition

Opponent strength is the opponents' opponent-adjusted net EPA per play, not their records. Tiers are league thirds of that rating. Results against tiers rest on two or three games each.

Depth, injuries and personnel

Confirmed absences (reserve list, Out/Doubtful, or inactive for each of the last three games) are kept apart from Questionable and unexplained absences. Absences are weighted by snap share, not by an invented point value. A report for a game the player then played is not counted.
