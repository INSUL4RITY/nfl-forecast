# QB evaluation, game state and spread attribution: audit and plan (2026-11-16)

Response to the user's brief "NFL model improvement: quarterback evaluation, garbage-time adjustments and fair spread
construction". This is the audit and proposal the brief asks for before any code changes. **Nothing here changes the
frozen 2026 model, the v2 trial line or any published forecast.** CLAUDE.md: the model is frozen and final for 2026;
a model change needs a new version, and no feature may be evaluated as "untouched" on seasons already seen. Everything
below is therefore work toward a 2027 candidate ("v3"), built in a worktree, with its pass rules written down before any
result is seen.

## 1. What the current model does (audited in code)

### Quarterback rating (`features/personnel.py`, `QBModel`)
* **Unit:** EPA per dropback from nflfastR play-by-play, per QB per game: `qb_dropback == 1`, spikes and no-plays
  excluded, so sacks and scrambles are included; designed QB runs are not. Raw `epa`, **not opponent-adjusted**, no
  game-state weighting.
* **Recency:** exponential weights with a 16-game half-life, times 0.8 per season back (`QB_HALF_LIFE_GAMES`,
  `QB_SEASON_CARRY`).
* **Sample size:** rating = (weighted EPA + 250 x prior) / (weighted dropbacks + 250) (`QB_SHRINK_DROPBACKS = 250`).
  The prior is as-of: league EPA/dropback over the last ~3 seasons, or the newcomer average (QBs with < 200 career
  dropbacks) when there are > 500 such dropbacks (else league - 0.1). So rookies and backups are already shrunk, toward a
  newcomer/replacement-level prior rather than to the team's starter. **The 250 / 16 / 0.8 constants are fixed in code;
  I found no record of them being tuned or validated** (the start-probability shrinkage in `qb_rates_eval.py` was, the
  rating's was not). Draft capital is not used.
* **Starter:** first dropback by an eligible passer (listed QB, or >= 5 dropbacks; audit fix 2026-11-13).
* **Features in the frozen ridge** (per team side): `qb_rating`, `qb_log_db` (log of weighted dropbacks = confidence),
  `qb_delta`, `qb_change`.
* **QB vs team separation already exists:** `qb_delta` = expected starter's rating minus `baseline_rating`, the rating of
  the QBs who started the games in the team's weighted history, with the same weights as team form. A team that used
  three starters has a baseline that mixes them, and the upcoming starter is scored relative to that mix. This is the
  brief's item 4 in principle. Measured on the 6,066 training snapshots: `qb_delta` is almost uncorrelated with the
  team's offensive form (|r| <= 0.06 with EPA/play, EPA/dropback, points), so the adjustment is identifiable rather than
  double counted. What it does not do: re-weight the baseline by how recent each QB's starts were *within* the
  team form windows used for each separate metric, or account for the supporting cast.
* **Measured weights** (research list B3): 7.3 pts per 1.0 EPA/db of rating, 20.9 per 1.0 of rating vs team baseline,
  -1.15 for any change of starter; starter-to-backup swaps in 2026 moved the line 4.3 pts on average, 8.3 at most.

### Feature groups and overlap ("double counting")
The frozen stats-only ridge (alpha 100 on standardised inputs) has 48 inputs: 18 offence and 18 defence form metrics
(EPA/play, EPA/dropback, EPA/rush, success, explosive pass/run, sack, interception and fumble rates, neutral pass rate,
points/drive, red-zone TD, starting field position, plays, points, opponent-adjusted EPA and points), 4 QB terms, rest,
venue, dome, playoff and games-played terms.
* **Overlap is large.** Among the 18 offence metrics the mean |correlation| is 0.36 and the first principal component
  holds 47% of their variance (3 components: 63%). `qb_rating` correlates 0.83 with team EPA/dropback, 0.80 with
  EPA/play, 0.79 with adjusted EPA, 0.73 with points per game.
* **This does not double count in the prediction:** the ridge fits all coefficients jointly, so a shared signal is paid
  once, with the weight spread across the correlated inputs (that is what the shrinkage is for). Where it does show is the
  **attribution bars**: how the shared "good offence" signal is split between Quarterbacks, Passing, Efficiency and
  Scoring is arbitrary between collinear inputs, so the split by group should not be read as separate effects.

### Attribution bars ("Where the model line comes from", `predict/breakdown.py`)
Already model-based and exact: each bar is the sum over a group of (home - away input, standardised) x the ridge
coefficient, averaged over QB scenarios by their probabilities; `football_breakdown` returns nothing unless the pieces
add to the published margin within 1e-6. So the brief's "must reconcile mathematically" already holds. Gaps: groups are
by statistic family, not by offence / defence / QB / special teams / availability; no drill-down to the individual
inputs; collinearity (above) makes group splits unstable.

### Game state ("garbage time") - already tested once
v2.0 (pre-registered 2026-10-26) included a **hard** filter: team efficiency stats used only plays with the offence's
pre-snap `wp` (nflfastR's non-market win probability) between 0.10 and 0.90. In the drop-one ablation over 2019-2025
(1,960 games) removing it **improved** the pooled squared margin error by 0.80 (the largest harmful group; travel/body
clock was the other). So "throw low-leverage plays away" failed. Untested: a **smooth** weight, applied to the **QB
rating** rather than team form, with the curve chosen on tune folds only. The brief's version is genuinely new.

### Already on the 2027 list (`docs/research_2027.md`)
B3 (opponent adjustment, designed runs, starter quality), B4 (CPOE, aDOT), B5 (QB x supporting cast), B7 (injuries by
player value), B10 (reserve lists), B11 (roster changes), B19 (coach/QB carry-over: failed pre-season), B29 (new QB after
a trade), A12 (EPA version change in nflfastR PR 618), A13/A14 (intervals and stricter pass rules).

## 3. Validation constraints (the honest part)

* **No untouched historical season is left.** 2025 was the locked test (used once, 2026-09-24) and 2019-2025 was pooled
  for the v2 tests. Development can use walk-forward 2016-2025 with tune folds, but any result there is "seen data".
* **The decisive test has to be prospective:** v3 predictions generated privately and RFC 3161-timestamped from a fixed
  date (e.g. week 12 of 2026), not shown on the site, then judged on the rest of 2026 (about 100 games) and on 2027.
  About 100 games cannot detect a 0.05-point improvement in average error, so realistically the promotion decision is
  for the 2027 season.
* **Pass rules written first** (research list A14): improvement must hold on the pooled walk-forward error with a 95%
  interval that excludes zero, or the feature stays out.
* **Leakage:** every new QB input is built through the existing as-of machinery (`kickoff + 12 h` availability); injury
  and practice inputs use only versions retrieved before the prediction time (we have these only from 2026).

## 4. Proposed experiments (in this order, each one a separate pre-registered comparison)

| | Experiment | What changes | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Baseline | Frozen v1.0 refit in the same harness (reproduce 12.98 RMSE on 2019-2025). | - |
| B1 | Opponent-adjusted QB rating | EPA/db adjusted for the defences faced (same method as `adj_off_epa`), designed runs added. | Small gain. |
| B2 | Tuned shrinkage | Choose the 250-dropback pseudo-count, half-life and carry on tune folds only; add a draft-capital prior for rookies (round/pick). | Few rookie starts per season. |
| C | Leverage-weighted QB rating | Weight each dropback by w(wp) (smooth: e.g. 1 for wp 0.2-0.8, tapering to a floor; also a logistic curve), curve and floor chosen on tune folds; keep the unweighted rating as a separate input so the ridge can choose. Report full-game, competitive, low-leverage and weighted EPA/db for every QB. | The hard filter already failed; a smooth weight may only recover noise. |
| D | B + C combined | | |
| E | Supporting-cast split | QB rating from air EPA + YAC over expected (`yac_epa - xyac_epa` credited to receivers) and pressure-adjusted EPA (2016+ pressure where present, else missing-indicator); B7/B10 availability inputs. | Data thin before 2023. |
| F | Overlap reduction | Same model with grouped inputs (offence / defence / QB / special teams / context) or a principal-component offence factor; keep only if error does not worsen. Mainly to make the bars stable and readable. | Error may rise slightly. |

Metrics for every row: margin MAE and RMSE, Brier and log loss of win probability, calibration, record against
untimed (~closing) lines with intervals, and splits by QB experience (< 200 career dropbacks, backups, traded QBs), by
games with a starter change, regular season vs playoffs.

Coaching (brief item 7): only measurable tendencies (`pass_oe`, pace, 4th-down rate, play-caller continuity), tested as a
separate group after A-F; no coach ratings. 4th-down rate already failed once.

## 5. Diagnostics and display (after a model passes)

* A per-QB card on game pages: expected starter, start probability (already shown), rating, dropbacks of evidence,
  confidence, full / competitive / low-leverage EPA per dropback, and the rating vs his team's baseline in points.
* Attribution regrouped into QB / offence / defence / special teams / availability / home / rest / situational, with a
  drill-down table of the individual inputs (each input's points), still reconciling exactly to the margin.
  This is display work and, like everything else here, would ship with the 2027 model, not into the frozen 2026 one.

## 6. Recommended order

1. Experiment A and C (leverage-weighted QB rating), because it is the user's top priority and cheap to build on
   existing pbp. 2. B (opponent adjustment, tuned shrinkage, draft prior). 3. E and F. 4. Coaching tendencies.
Start in the off-season or in a worktree now with predictions logged privately; nothing reaches the live model or site
before a pre-registered test passes.
